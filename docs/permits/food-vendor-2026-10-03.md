### What the State requires (this one has a date)

| Requirement | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Existing Maine food-service license | A local restaurant that already holds one needs nothing extra; verify it is current | doc 1044 Finding 3 |
| Temporary Food Service License (Maine DHHS Form HHE-602) for a vendor without one | **Must be submitted 30 days before the event: Thursday 3 September 2026.** DHHS does not process late applications. Cap of 3 temporary licenses per vendor per year | doc 1044 Finding 3, sourced to the Maine DHHS forms page |
| Vendor liability insurance | Standard practice is the vendor carries their own and issues a COI naming ZAOstock as additional insured | doc 1045 Finding 4 |
| Written verification before setup | The organiser's responsibility, not only the vendor's | doc 1044 Key Decisions |

**What the 3 September date means today (27 August):** a vendor without a
license has seven days to file. A local restaurant with a license has no
deadline from the State. So the only vendors realistically in reach are ones
already licensed - which is doc 1044's recommendation anyway (recruit local, not
a food-truck circuit; no booth fee in year one).

### What the Chamber can do

The Ellsworth Area Chamber of Commerce does not permit anything; it is "the front
door to Ellsworth's business network" (the CRM's words). The ask to them is a
pointer, not a permission: which member restaurant or food truck would pop up on
the parklet for a free downtown festival, and does the Chamber have a members'
channel to put that ask in.
