## The three options

| Tier | A - Local ladder | B - Steep ladder | C - Flat ladder |
|------|------------------|------------------|-----------------|
| Presenting | $2,500 | $5,000 | $1,500 |
| Platform | $1,000 | $2,000 | $1,000 |
| Sponsor an artist | $500 | $500 | $500 |
| Community | $250 | $250 | $500 |
| Friend of the Fest | $50 | $50 | $50 |
| Top to bottom spread | 50x | 100x | 30x |
| Realistic top buyer | A local bank | A regional or crypto-side sponsor | Several mid-size local businesses |

### A - Local ladder, $2,500 top. **Recommended.**

Built for the audience we can actually reach. The realistic Presenting and
Platform candidates are the banks, and they already sponsor things in this town:
Franklin Savings with its new Ellsworth branch, Bangor Federal Credit Union and
Machias Savings are both named Art of Ellsworth sponsors, First National, and
Sealander Architects who sponsor Art of Ellsworth directly. All warm through
Heart of Ellsworth.

$2,500 is a number a branch manager can approve out of a local marketing budget
without escalating. That is the whole argument. We are not cold-pitching, we are
asking businesses that already sponsor the umbrella event this one sits inside.

Risk: if someone bigger wants in, we have left money on the table. Mitigated by
first refusal on 2027 sitting inside Presenting.

### B - Steep ladder, $5,000 top

Right if there is a non-local sponsor in play, or if this deck is also going to
anyone outside Ellsworth. A $5,000 top makes $1,000 read as the modest middle
option rather than a serious commitment, which pulls the middle of the ladder up
with it.

Risk: $5,000 escalates past a branch manager almost everywhere in town. If the
banks are the real buyers, this ladder gets one polite no from each of them and
we discover it in mid-September.

### C - Flat ladder, $1,500 top

Right if the goal is several sponsors rather than one anchor. Collapses
Community and Sponsor-an-artist into the same $500 price, so the ladder has
three real rungs instead of five and every conversation is shorter.

Risk: no headline sponsor, so the banner and the stage have no single name on
them, and there is nothing to offer first refusal on 2027.

## The discount rules, unchanged from doc 2326

They hold whichever option is picked.

- **Early close.** 25% off any cash tier for a signed yes by the date below.
- **In-kind** counts at retail value toward a tier. No extra discount on top.
  Keeps the Arbor Camp, food and print pitches to one sentence.
- **Sponsor-an-artist** is flat $500 and never discounted.
- **Mutuals floor** is $50, no negotiation.

## The early-close date

**Recommended: Friday 11 September 2026.**

This is the one field that cannot be left blank. A deck with a deadline gets
answered. A deck without one gets read and put down.

Why the 11th and not later. Sponsor logos have to be on the poster, the poster
cannot start until set times exist, and Candy's print list is due 30 August. A
yes arriving after the poster goes to print buys a website logo and nothing
physical, which is not what $2,500 was sold as. Working back from print, the
11th is the last Friday that still gets a new sponsor onto printed material.

It also sits ten days after the 1 September lineup reveal, which is the moment
the deck is most persuasive - a sponsor reading it will have just seen the
lineup announcement.

| Alternative | Case for it | Case against |
|-------------|-------------|--------------|
| Friday 4 September | Only three days after the reveal, so it is the sharpest possible deadline | Three days is not enough time for a bank to route an approval. Likely produces nos that would have been yeses |
| **Friday 11 September** | Ten days post-reveal, still makes print | Nothing significant |
| Friday 25 September | Catches late deciders | Past print. Anyone signing then gets digital-only, so the tier is worth less than they paid |
