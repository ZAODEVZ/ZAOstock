## Why the earlier draft was wrong, in one paragraph

It attributed two anchors - a $500 sponsor-an-artist tier and a $50 floor - to
Zaal, sourced to "doc 2326". Doc 2326 is
`research/events/2326-zaostock-sponsor-leads-tiers` and it does **not** contain
those numbers as Zaal's decisions. It is a leads-tiering doc: it names prospects,
assigns owners, and uses the tier **names**. The prices came from a clipboard
draft that asserted the provenance, and the draft was believed instead of
checked. `content/pitch-pack/deck-words.md:348` even flags its own ladder as
something to "reset", and `docs/audit/2026-05-12-public-surfaces.md:181` recorded
the $500-$2,500 range as INCONSISTENT back in May. The figures have been floating
unvalidated for three months and got laundered into a recommendation.

This is the same failure as citing a doc nobody can point at a line for. A
number with a source that does not say it is worse than a number with no source,
because it reads as settled.

## The one piece of real reasoning worth keeping

Whatever the early-close date turns out to be, it is **constrained by print, not
chosen freely.** Sponsor logos go on the poster. The poster cannot start until
set times exist. The print list is a Candy deliverable. So the last useful
early-close date is the last date a new sponsor still reaches printed material,
and after that a yes buys a website logo and nothing physical - which is not
what a top tier is sold as.

That is a constraint Zaal can use to pick a date. **It is not a date.** The
earlier draft turned this reasoning into "Friday 11 September" and then called
it recommended, which is exactly the step that was not ours to take.
